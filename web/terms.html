CompanionChat

Terms of use

Short, because a document nobody reads protects nobody. These are the rules of using CompanionChat, and what happens when they are broken.

CompanionChat is peer support, not therapy. The people who listen are not licensed clinicians and nothing here is medical advice.

Who can use it

You must be 18 or older. If you apply to be someone who listens, we ask for a date of birth and compute an age from it — the date itself is never shown to anyone.

What you are paying for

Tokens. One token sends one message. You begin with 50 and receive 25 free every day. There is currently nothing to buy, and if that changes the price will be shown before anything is charged.

A token spent on a message that is never answered is refunded automatically. That is a rule in the database, not a support policy.

What is not allowed

  • Sexual content, solicitation, or requests for sexual services.
  • Harassment, threats, hate, or contacting someone who has blocked you.
  • Asking for or sharing contact details to move the conversation off the app.
  • Impersonating someone else, or presenting yourself as a medical or legal professional.
  • Anything involving a minor. There is no second chance on this one.

Blocking is immediate and mutual. Reports go to a person, not a queue that nobody reads. Accounts that break these rules are suspended, and the conversation history is what we look at — which is why reports attach a copy of the specific message.

If you are in crisis

Please contact a helpline or emergency services. The app shows numbers on its safety screen. The people here listen; they are not equipped for an emergency and it is not fair to either of you to treat them as if they are.

Ending it

You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app, in two taps — see deleting your data. We can suspend an account for breaking the rules above. Abuse reports survive a deletion for 90 days with the reporter's identity removed, and that exception is explained on the privacy page.

What we do not promise

That someone will always be available, that a conversation will help, or that the service will be uninterrupted. This is a small operation and it is better to say so than to write a warranty nobody could honour.
